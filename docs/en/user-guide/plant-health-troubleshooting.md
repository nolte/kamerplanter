# My Plant Doesn't Look Well — What Now?

Yellow leaves, brown tips, sticky residue, or a droopy plant? This page helps you get a first read on what's happening. Based on the visible symptom, you'll find the right next step to take in Kamerplanter.

!!! info "Looking for a different kind of troubleshooting?"
    This page covers **plant health** (leaves, growth, pests). If you're looking for help with a technical problem in the Kamerplanter application itself (installation, login, error messages), see [Operations Troubleshooting](../development/troubleshooting.md) instead.

---

## Before you check the table

A few quick questions often help before you even start looking for a cause:

- **How long has the plant looked like this?** A single old, lower leaf turning yellow is usually just normal leaf aging — not a disease.
- **Has anything changed recently?** A new location, new pot, new substrate, a change in watering habits, or a new fertilizer are common triggers.
- **How does the substrate feel?** Stick a finger 2–3 cm (about an inch) into it. Many symptoms are directly related to too much or too little water.

If you're unsure whether it's a pest: take a photo and use [pest detection](pest-detection.md) — that's usually the fastest way to get a well-founded assessment.

## Leaves: Discoloration and Spots

| Symptom | Possible Cause | First Action | Learn More |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|------------|
| Yellow leaves, mainly lower/inner, evenly across the whole leaf | Natural leaf aging, nitrogen deficiency, or improper watering (too wet or too dry) | Check the substrate moisture first. If that looks fine, review your fertilization plan | [Fertilization](fertilization.md), [Watering Log](watering-log.md) |
| Yellow leaves, but the leaf veins stay noticeably green | Nutrient deficiency (often iron or magnesium), frequently caused by an unfavorable pH in the nutrient solution | Measure the [EC](../reference/glossary.md#electrical-conductivity-ec) and pH of your nutrient solution and compare them with the target values for the current phase | [Fertilization](fertilization.md), [Sensors and Measurement Data](sensors.md) |
| Brown, dry leaf tips or edges | Low humidity, hard tap water with high mineral content, or fertilizer concentration too high (salt stress) | Reduce fertilizer concentration as a test, and check whether you're using hard tap water | [Fertilization](fertilization.md), [Care Reminders](care-reminders.md) |
| Brown or black spots with a border, often on older leaves | Fungal infection (e.g. leaf spot disease) or sunburn from a sudden increase in light intensity | Remove affected leaves, take a photo, and use pest detection to narrow down a fungal cause | [Pest Detection by Photo](pest-detection.md), [Integrated Pest Management (IPM)](pest-management.md) |
| White, powdery coating on the top or underside of leaves | Powdery mildew (fungal disease), favored by high humidity combined with poor air circulation | Take a photo and use pest detection; don't wipe off affected areas (this spreads spores) | [Pest Detection by Photo](pest-detection.md), [Integrated Pest Management (IPM)](pest-management.md) |
| Curled, deformed young leaves or shoot tips | Aphids or other pests that prefer soft, young tissue; less commonly a nutrient deficiency | Inspect new growth closely (including the underside of leaves), take a photo, and use pest detection | [Pest Detection by Photo](pest-detection.md) |
| Several leaves drop at the same time within a short period | Location shock from a draft, sudden temperature change, or a strong change in light (e.g. after moving the plant) | Check location conditions: avoid drafts, temperature swings, and sudden changes in light | [Sensors and Measurement Data](sensors.md), [Locations and Substrates](locations-substrates.md) |

## Pests and Signs of Infestation

| Symptom | Possible Cause | First Action | Learn More |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|------------|
| Sticky leaves, or a sticky residue on leaves, furniture, or the floor under the plant | Honeydew — excreted by sap-sucking pests such as aphids, scale insects, or whiteflies | Inspect the underside of leaves closely, take a photo, and use pest detection | [Pest Detection by Photo](pest-detection.md), [Integrated Pest Management (IPM)](pest-management.md) |
| Fine, silvery or light speckling on the leaf surface, fine webbing on the underside | Spider mites — hard to spot as insects with the naked eye, often favored by dry air | Increase humidity, take a photo, and use pest detection (it also works without a visible insect) | [Pest Detection by Photo](pest-detection.md), [Integrated Pest Management (IPM)](pest-management.md) |
| Small black flies hovering around the pot or flying up when watering | Fungus gnats — their larvae live in constantly moist substrate, especially in soil and coco | Let the substrate surface dry out between waterings, set out yellow sticky traps | [Integrated Pest Management (IPM)](pest-management.md), [Care Reminders](care-reminders.md) |
| Small, often greenish or black insects clustered on shoot tips and leaf undersides | Aphids | Take a photo and use pest detection to assess species and severity | [Pest Detection by Photo](pest-detection.md), [Integrated Pest Management (IPM)](pest-management.md) |
| Tiny white moths that swarm up when the plant is touched | Whiteflies | Take a photo and use pest detection | [Pest Detection by Photo](pest-detection.md), [Integrated Pest Management (IPM)](pest-management.md) |

!!! danger "Don't accidentally treat a beneficial insect as a pest"
    Not every crawling thing on your plant is a pest. Predatory beneficials such as ladybug larvae, lacewings, or predatory mites can look unusual but are helpful. [Pest detection](pest-detection.md) explicitly flags beneficials as such instead of reporting them as pests.

## Growth Form, Substrate, and Other Symptoms

| Symptom | Possible Cause | First Action | Learn More |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|------------|
| Wilted, droopy plant even though the substrate is moist to wet | Root rot from waterlogging or poor drainage, less commonly a root pest | Check substrate moisture and, if possible, the roots; water less and less often going forward | [Locations and Substrates](locations-substrates.md), [Watering Log](watering-log.md) |
| Wilted, droopy plant with dry, hard substrate | Too little or too irregular watering | Water thoroughly, then set up a suitable watering interval via care reminders | [Care Reminders](care-reminders.md), [Watering Log](watering-log.md) |
| Whitish, crusty coating on the substrate surface or pot rim | Mineral buildup from hard tap water, or a fertilizer concentration that's been too high for too long | Check water quality, try watering with rainwater or reverse-osmosis water as a test, and reduce fertilizer concentration | [Fertilization](fertilization.md), [Sensors and Measurement Data](sensors.md) |
| Very slow growth, or long, thin, pale shoots (etiolation) | Not enough light for the current growth phase | Check light intensity and duration; move the plant to a brighter location if needed | [Sensors and Measurement Data](sensors.md) |
| No flowering even though the plant looks healthy | Wrong photoperiod, mismatched phase settings, or — for biennial species — missing cold exposure (vernalization) | Check the plant's phase settings and photoperiod | [Growth Phases](growth-phases.md) |

## If You Can't Find the Symptom or Aren't Sure

1. Take a sharp, well-lit photo of the affected area and use [pest detection](pest-detection.md) — it recognizes both visible pests and typical damage patterns, even when no insect is visible.
2. If there's a confirmed infestation, log an inspection in the [pest management (IPM) system](pest-management.md) to track it over time.
3. Check whether anything has changed recently in your watering habits, location, or fertilization — your [care reminders](care-reminders.md) and [watering log](watering-log.md) can help you trace that back.

## Frequently Asked Questions

??? question "I don't see an insect, but the leaves look infested — what now?"
    Many pests, such as spider mites or thrips, are nearly impossible to spot with the naked eye. Use [pest detection](pest-detection.md): it also recognizes pure damage patterns (webbing, speckling, sucking damage) without the causing insect needing to be visible in the photo.

??? question "Is a single yellow leaf already a cause for concern?"
    Usually not. Plants naturally shed their oldest, lowest leaves over time — that's normal leaf aging. Keep observing the plant; a closer check is worthwhile only once several leaves at once, or young leaves, are affected.

??? question "How do I know whether it's related to watering or fertilization?"
    A look at your [watering log](watering-log.md) shows you when and how much was last watered, and whether EC or pH values were recorded. If a value deviates noticeably from the target ranges in [fertilization](fertilization.md), that's a strong indicator.

??? question "Can Kamerplanter automatically detect that my plant isn't doing well?"
    Kamerplanter does not currently evaluate general disease symptoms automatically. What is evaluated automatically are photos you specifically submit via [pest detection](pest-detection.md). For everything else, this page is meant as a reference guide.
