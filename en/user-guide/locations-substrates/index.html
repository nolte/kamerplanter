    Locations and Substrates¶

    Locations describe where your plants grow — from your entire garden down to a single pot slot. Substrates define the growing medium. Both concepts form the spatial foundation for all other features in Kamerplanter.

    Prerequisites¶

    • A Kamerplanter account with at least one tenant (created automatically during onboarding)
    • For substrates: at least one location already set up

    Understanding the Location Hierarchy¶

    Kamerplanter organises locations in a tree structure with three levels:

    Site (facility)
  └── Location (area)
        └── Slot (plant spot)

    Site is your top-level facility — for example "My Garden" or "Berlin Apartment". At the site level you store the water source, climate zone, and total area.

    Location is a concrete area within a site — for example "Grow Tent A", "Raised Bed 1", or "South Balcony". Locations can contain further locations: you can model "House" → "Living Room" → "South Window Sill".

    Slot is a single planting spot — for example "TENT01_A1" for spot A1 in grow tent 1. Slots are always the bottom level and can be assigned to exactly one plant.

    How deep should the structure be?

    For simple setups (balcony, one grow tent) it is sufficient to create sites and locations. Slots are useful when you have many plants in the same area and want to track each spot individually.

    Creating a New Site¶

    Step 1: Navigate to Location Management¶

    Click Locations in the left navigation. The overview page shows all your sites.

    Step 2: Create a New Site¶

    Click Add Site (top right). A form opens.

    Step 3: Fill in Basic Data¶

    Field Description Example
    Name Site name "My Indoor Garden"
    Type Determines which features are available for this site (see table below) "Outdoor"
    Latitude / Longitude (GPS) GPS coordinates of the site — fill in both fields together or leave both empty 52.52 / 13.40
    Climate Zone Location climate zone in USDA hardiness-zone format "8a"
    Total Area (m²) Total growing area 12
    Timezone Timezone for tasks and calendar "Europe/Berlin"

    Available site types:

    Type Description
    Outdoor An open-air site — unlocks weather sources and overwintering automation
    Greenhouse Glass house or poly tunnel — unlocks weather sources and overwintering automation
    Indoor A room or living space with no direct outdoor climate exposure
    Windowsill A window spot with indirect outdoor climate exposure
    Balcony Balcony — a frost-exposed outdoor site: unlocks GPS coordinates, weather sources, and overwintering automation, just like Outdoor and Greenhouse
    Grow Tent Enclosed grow tent with controlled climate

    Why USDA zones and not Köppen climate classification?

    Kamerplanter expects the climate zone in USDA Plant Hardiness Zone format (a number from 1–13, optionally with an "a" or "b" suffix, e.g. "8a"), not the Köppen climate classification (e.g. "Cfb"). The reason: the hardiness data for plant species in the master data (hardiness_zones) uses the same format — this is what later allows Kamerplanter to check automatically whether a species can overwinter outdoors at your location. You can look up the matching zone for your area via the official USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map or comparable hardiness zone maps for your region.

    Why GPS coordinates matter

    Only once GPS coordinates are stored can Kamerplanter automatically fetch weather data and calculate frost warnings for Outdoor, Greenhouse, and Balcony sites — a balcony counts as a frost-exposed outdoor location and is therefore treated exactly like Outdoor and Greenhouse. If you enter coordinates for a different type (e.g. Indoor) anyway, they remain unused for now — if you later switch the type to Outdoor, Greenhouse, or Balcony, they take effect automatically.

    The climate zone can be derived automatically

    For Outdoor and Greenhouse sites with GPS coordinates, Kamerplanter already computes this zone automatically in the background from your site's long-term climate data and keeps this field in sync — you don't have to look it up yourself in that case. For details on the computation, manually overriding it, and the current state of the web interface, see Climate Zones & Hardiness.

    Experience levels

    Depending on your experience level (Beginner / Intermediate / Expert, configurable in account settings) you will see more or fewer fields. Name and type are already visible at Beginner level; GPS coordinates, water source configuration, climate zone, and total area are added from Intermediate level upward; the timezone is an Expert-level field.

    Step 4: Configure Water Source (optional, Intermediate and above)¶

    If you use tap water or a reverse osmosis system, enter the water values. The system will automatically calculate your EC budget (EC = electrical conductivity, a measure of the nutrient-salt concentration of your solution — see Fertilization for details), CalMag requirements, and mixing recommendations.

    Tap Water Profile¶

    Field Unit Typical Range Description
    EC mS/cm 0.3–0.8 Electrical conductivity — indicates total mineral content
    pH — 7.0–8.0 Acidity of the water
    General Hardness (GH) ppm CaCO3 100–350 Sum of dissolved minerals (Ca + Mg)
    Carbonate Hardness (KH) ppm CaCO3 80–250 Buffer capacity of the water (Alkalinity)
    Calcium (Ca) mg/L 30–120 Important for CalMag calculation
    Magnesium (Mg) mg/L 5–30 Important for CalMag calculation
    Chlorine mg/L 0–0.3 At > 0.1 mg/L, let water stand or filter
    Chloramine mg/L 0 Rarely used in Europe

    Additional Options¶

    • Has RO system: Enable this if you have a reverse osmosis unit. The system will then calculate mixing ratios for tap and RO water.
    • Measurement date: Date of the water analysis. Kamerplanter warns you if the analysis is older than 12 months.
    • Source note: Free text for the origin of the values (e.g. "City Water Report 2025").

    Finding your water values

    Your local water utility typically provides a free drinking water analysis — often as a PDF download on their website. In many countries, water suppliers are legally required to publish these values.

    Examples (Germany):

    • Hamburg: hamburgwasser.de/wasser — postal code lookup under "Mein Trinkwasser"
    • Berlin: berliner-wasserbetriebe.de — water quality by postal code
    • Munich: swm.de — drinking water analysis by supply area

    Alternatively, you can measure the values yourself: an EC/TDS meter (TDS = Total Dissolved Solids; from approx. 15 EUR) provides the EC value, a pH meter the pH. For calcium and magnesium, drop tests (GH/KH test kits from aquarium supplies, from approx. 8 EUR) are an affordable option.

    Why accurate water values matter

    Kamerplanter uses your water values to calculate the EC budget (how much room is left for fertilizer) and the CalMag correction (whether additional calcium/magnesium is needed). Inaccurate values lead to wrong fertilization recommendations — in the worst case, over- or under-fertilization.

    Step 5: Save¶

    Click Save. The site appears in the overview.

    For technical users

    Besides name, type, GPS coordinates, climate zone, area, and timezone, a site also tracks average frost dates (last spring frost, first autumn frost, and the German "Eisheilige" date) in the background. This setting is currently only available via the API — it is not yet editable in the site form. The benefit: once a site has a GPS position, Kamerplanter can calculate the actual day length at your location and correctly evaluate automatic, photoperiod-triggered phase transitions (e.g. flower onset for outdoor short-day plants) — see Automatic Phase Transitions. Frost dates also feed into the sowing calendar.

    GPS coordinates unlock weather sources

    For sites of type Outdoor, Greenhouse, or Balcony, a stored GPS position also unlocks the Weather Source section on the site detail page — there you select and prioritize public weather services or a Home Assistant source, see Weather Sources per Location.

    Sun and reach for garden planning¶

    When the garden planner module is switched on, the detail page of a site has the section Garden planning: sun and reach. The values apply to every bed of the site.

    Setting Default Effect
    Full sun: more than 6 h help text of the Full sun tile
    Partial shade: from 3 h help text of the Partial shade tile (up to the full-sun value)
    Shade: from 2 h help text of the Shade tile; below it is Deep shade
    Reference date (day, month) 21 June the day the hours of direct sun refer to
    Access from two sides 1.2 m guide value of the Bed too wide hint with two opposite access sides
    Access from one side 0.6 m guide value with one access side, and when no access sides are recorded

    The sun defaults follow the Royal Horticultural Society, the reach defaults arm length (Oregon State and Iowa State University Extension, the environment ministry of Rhineland-Palatinate). Every value must be greater than 0. The sun hours must decrease from top to bottom (full sun above partial shade above shade), and the one-side value may not be greater than the two-side value. Otherwise the form names the error at the field and saves nothing.

    Kamerplanter computes nothing else from these values: you still choose the sun class of a bed yourself, the values only decide what the tiles show as the definition. If you do not change them, the defaults stay in force.

    For technical users

    In the API the fields are sun_class_thresholds (full_sun_min_hours, partial_shade_min_hours, shade_min_hours, reference_month, reference_day) and reach_thresholds (both_sides_m, one_side_m) on PUT /api/v1/t/{tenant_slug}/sites/{key}. A field missing from the request, or null, keeps the stored value; invalid values are answered with 422. The response always carries the effective values, that is the defaults while nothing is stored.

    Adding Locations and Slots¶

    Adding a Location Within a Site¶

    1. Open a site by clicking its name.
    2. In the Locations tab you see the location tree.
    3. Click Add Location.
    4. Select a location type from the list (see table below).
    5. Enter a unique name.
    6. Optional: Select a parent location (for nested structures).

    Available Location Types:

    Type Indoor? Description
    Zone — Free-form subdivision without a fixed assignment, e.g. for rough area planning
    Home No Top level for a private residence
    Garden No The entire outdoor area
    Greenhouse No Glass house or poly tunnel
    Building Yes A building as an area, e.g. an outbuilding or shed
    Room Yes A whole room as an area
    Balcony No Balcony
    Terrace No Terrace
    Grow Tent Yes Enclosed grow tent with controlled climate
    Bed No Ground-level bed outdoors
    Raised Bed No Elevated, framed bed
    Greenhouse Bed No Bed inside a greenhouse
    Cold Frame No Covered box for early propagation
    Planter No Movable planter or a group of pots planned like a bed
    Shelf Yes Shelf or shelving unit
    Container Group No Grouping of several pots or containers in one place

    For technical users

    The sixteen types listed above are pre-installed system types. Kamerplanter already supports custom, additional location types internally. This setting is currently only available via the API — there is no dedicated management page for it in the UI yet.

    Setting Frost Exposure for a Location¶

    Whether a plant counts as frost-exposed normally depends on the type of the parent site: Outdoor, Greenhouse, and Balcony are automatically treated as frost-exposed, while Indoor, Windowsill, and Grow Tent are not (see Available site types). For each individual location within a site, you can override this classification in the Frost exposure field — useful, for example, when a single location differs from the rest of the site.

    The form for creating or editing a location offers three options:

    Option Effect
    Inherit from site (default) Uses the parent site type's classification — matches previous behaviour with no override.
    Outdoor – frost-exposed The location counts as frost-exposed regardless of the site type.
    Indoor – protected The location counts as protected, even if the parent site is set up as Outdoor, Greenhouse, or Balcony.

    A location's frost exposure determines both whether plants at that location receive an overwintering plan and whether they're covered by season automation — winter approach, dormancy care, and spring reactivation. Both follow the same classification consistently: a location that counts as frost-exposed gets the full automation, even if it sits on an otherwise indoor site; a location classified as protected is never put into winter dormancy, even if other locations on the same site are frost-exposed.

    A balcony underneath a heated apartment

    If your balcony sits underneath another, heated apartment, it is often noticeably warmer than a free-standing balcony. Kamerplanter still marks it as frost-exposed by default, since the site is set up with type "Balcony". If you want to override this for just this one location, open it and select "Indoor – protected" under Frost exposure — plants at this location then no longer receive an overwintering plan, even though other areas of the same site remain frost-exposed. Conversely, you can mark a particularly exposed location (e.g. a free-standing window sill in an unheated stairwell) as frost-exposed even when the parent site is set up as Indoor.

    Undoing an override at any time

    When editing a location, you can switch freely between all three options at any time — including back to "Inherit from site". This removes the override entirely; the location then automatically follows the parent site type's classification again.

    Adding a Slot Within a Location¶

    1. Open a location by clicking its name in the tree.
    2. Click Add Slot.
    3. Kamerplanter automatically suggests a slot ID in the format AREA_POSITION (e.g. "TENT01_A1"); you can adjust it, and it is automatically converted to uppercase on save.
    4. Enter the capacity — the maximum number of plants this slot can hold at once (1–20, default: 1).

    Managing Substrates¶

    A substrate describes the growing medium in which your plants root. Kamerplanter distinguishes between 14 substrate types, supports custom substrate mixes, and manages concrete batches of a substrate (e.g. "Organic soil, mixed March 2026") separately from the general substrate type.

    Creating a New Substrate¶

    1. Navigate to Locations → Substrates.
    2. Click Add Substrate.
    3. Select the substrate type (see table).
    4. Enter a name (German and English, e.g. "Organic Soil" / "Bio-Erde").
    5. Optional: Enter base pH, base EC, water retention, air porosity, and buffer capacity.

    Available Substrate Types:

    Type Description
    Soil Standard garden or potting soil
    Coco Coconut substrate (coco coir)
    Clay Pebbles Expanded clay pellets, mostly used in hydro systems
    Perlite Volcanic mineral, usually added for drainage
    Living Soil Soil with an active microbiome
    Peat Peat-based substrate
    Rockwool Slab Mineral wool slab for hydroponics
    Rockwool Plug Small mineral wool propagation cube for cuttings and germination
    Vermiculite Expanded mineral, usually added or used for propagation
    No Substrate For substrate-less systems (e.g. pure aeroponics)
    Orchid Bark Coarse bark chunks for epiphytes
    PON Mineral Substrate Mineral semi-hydro substrate (LECA-like)
    Sphagnum Moss Peat moss, commonly used for orchids and carnivorous plants
    Hydro Solution Pure nutrient solution with no solid substrate (e.g. DWC)

    Coco and CalMag

    Coco substrate actively binds calcium and magnesium. CalMag supplements are always recommended for coco substrates, even with hard tap water. Kamerplanter will warn you if a nutrient plan for coco plants contains no CalMag.

    Creating Custom Substrate Mixes¶

    Instead of using a single substrate type, you can combine several existing substrates into a custom mix (e.g. 70% soil + 20% perlite + 10% vermiculite):

    1. Click Create Mix in the substrate overview.
    2. Select at least two existing substrates (pure mixes cannot themselves be mixed again).
    3. Distribute the shares in percent — Distribute Evenly splits them automatically. The total must add up to exactly 100%.
    4. Click Preview to see the mix's calculated properties (base pH, base EC, water retention, air porosity, etc. — each as a weighted average of the components).
    5. Enter a name (German/English) and click Save.

    Substrate Batches (Reuse, Assignment)¶

    A batch is a concrete, physical quantity of a substrate with its own history — for example, a specific bag of soil reused across several growing cycles. For each batch, Kamerplanter tracks:

    Field Description
    Batch ID Freely chosen label, e.g. "SOIL-2026-03"
    Volume (litres) Amount of this batch
    Mixed on Date of preparation/purchase
    Last amended Date of the last nutrient/pH refresh
    Cycles used How many growing cycles this batch has already been used for
    Current pH / EC Latest measured values, including history

    Preparing for reuse: After completing a growing cycle, check whether a batch can be used again:

    1. Open the substrate batch in the detail view.
    2. Click Check Reusability. The system compares the batch's history against the substrate type's allowed reuse cycles.
    3. If preparation is needed, Kamerplanter shows the required steps (e.g. flushing, re-fertilizing) along with an estimated duration and the earliest date the batch will be ready again.
    4. Click Prepare for Reuse to log the preparation step.

    For technical users

    Kamerplanter can already technically link a substrate batch to a slot. This setting is currently only available via the API — there is no UI for this yet. Until then, enter the substrate reference in the Substrate Batch field when creating a planting run (see Planting Runs).

    Bed View¶

    If you open a location whose type is a bed (Bed, Raised Bed, Greenhouse Bed, Cold Frame or Planter), Kamerplanter shows the bed view instead of the regular location page. It brings everything important about a bed together in one place.

    Summary above the tabs¶

    Above the tabs you see at a glance: area, type, status, soil type, sun exposure, active plants, open tasks, last care, next due date and who is responsible for the bed. If the bed has a cover photo, it leads the summary (see Photos of the bed). Use the season selector to switch the year; it appears in the address as ?year=. Next to it you see what grew in the bed the year before.

    Nutrient hint. Under Nutrients the summary shows Fertiliser likely needed or Check feeding as soon as Kamerplanter can say something about it. Below it you see why: how long ago your last feeding or soil amendment with manure or horn shavings was (compost supplies little nitrogen and does not count), measured against the feeder level of the species in the bed, and what your lab report says about phosphorus and potassium. If you mulched with bark mulch after your last feeding, it says that bark mulch can bind nitrogen while it rots. If the previous crop was, or the current crop is, a nitrogen fixer such as beans or peas, the hint mentions it but does not estimate a nitrogen amount. If your lab report shows the soil is rich in phosphorus or potassium, compost serves no nutrient supply; as a soil improver it remains an option, and the hint never points you to compost or a phosphorus fertiliser. Only a lab report by the CAL method counts for the content classes. The hint never names a number or a fertiliser amount. When everything looks fine or data is missing, the line stays out.

    Partially available: nutrient hint

    The rules of the hint are in place. To show anything, every threshold (for example after how many days without feeding a heavy feeder runs short) needs a sourced value. No threshold has such a source yet, so the line stays empty for every bed for now. Once sourced values exist, Kamerplanter will show the hint.

    The tabs¶

    The active tab is part of the address (e.g. #plan or #soil), so you can share links to a specific tab.

    Tab Content
    Plan Bed frame with places, rows or zones and plant markers. Tap a plant and the bed offers Correct position and Transplant to …. Open in garden plan takes you to the garden plan for editing the bed frame.
    Care Open and overdue tasks of the bed and its plants, completing them, suggestions and the care log (see Care on the bed).
    Planting Planned and standing plants of the bed, the Plant and Clear bed buttons and the actions on a plant (see Planting a bed and Clearing a bed).
    History A timeline of what stood in the bed in earlier years, and the list of all care entries and plantings (see History of the bed).
    Soil The bed's soil profile and soil analyses (see Maintaining the soil profile and Recording soil analyses).
    Photos The bed's photos with its cover photo (see Photos of the bed).
    Details The previous location page, unchanged.

    Planting a bed¶

    On the Planting tab, Plant opens the planting dialog. It is available from the "grower" role and needs the bed to be drawn on the garden plan. The bed must not be retired.

    1. Choose species and cultivar. Your favourites come first. Below the choice you see the species' mature height and width and whether it needs a support.
    2. Read the hints. As soon as you have chosen a species, Kamerplanter checks crop rotation and the neighbours in the bed. Every hint has a strength (no issue, info, warning, critical) and a reason, for example "The family Solanaceae stood in this bed 1 year(s) ago. The rotation pause is 4 years". A hint blocks nothing. A site hint comes with them, see Sun and shade of a bed.
    3. Choose number and layout. Either As many as fit or a fixed number. The layout is Grid, Rows, Staggered, Zone (for direct sowing) or Free positions (for woody plants).
    4. Check the spacings. Kamerplanter prefills the plant spacing and row spacing from the species profile and says where the value comes from. If the profile has none, Kamerplanter uses the value you used last for this species. If there is none either, the field stays empty and you enter the spacing yourself. Under More options you set the row direction and the edge margins.
    5. Look at the preview. The bed shows at once where the plants would stand, and below it you see how many there are and how many fit at most. The server confirms the number a moment later. If its answer differs, its answer counts.
    6. Plan or record. Plan planting creates a planned run and reserves the places from the planting date. If you have planted already, switch on I have planted already. The plants are then created at once on the calculated positions.

    Several species (mixed culture). Add another species adds up to four species to the same planting. If all are laid out in rows, Kamerplanter spreads the rows alternately over the species, say carrot in every second row with onion in between. The row and edge settings of species 1 then apply to all; only the spacing within the row stays per species. Every row belongs to exactly one species: if two species claim the same row, Kamerplanter does not create the planting. If Kamerplanter rates a combination as incompatible, a hint appears.

    Free positions. With Free positions you tap the planting places onto the bed. Dragging moves a place, a number below the preview deletes it. Every place snaps to the garden plan's grid when you tap or drag it; hold Alt to place it freely to the centimetre. If two places are closer together than the minimum spacing, both get a yellow ring. That is a hint; you can still plant.

    Plant anyway. From the strength warning on, you switch on Plant anyway and give a reason. The reason is stored with the planting, and the bed carries the Crop rotation hint in its overview for as long as the planting runs.

    Pot volume. For species with a recommended pot volume you can enter the volume of your pot under More options. If it is smaller than recommended, Kamerplanter warns you.

    Correct position. For a standing plant (on the Planting tab or after a tap on the plan), Correct position moves its place: by dragging, with the arrow buttons in steps of 10 cm, or by typing the coordinates. For fine work, switch Step size to 5 cm or 1 cm. The care log records the correction together with the plant. Plants in a zone have no single place and cannot be moved.

    Transplant to … You do not move a plant to another bed by dragging. Transplant to … creates the "Transplant" task for the plant, with the target bed in its text. You complete it once you have moved the plant.

    Not yet implemented

    The Crop rotation hint appears only in the bed overview so far, not on the bed in the garden plan. It is set only for plantings you started despite a hint. Planned, still empty places appear on the plan as dashed circles without species and date. Your browser, not your account, remembers the spacings you used last. A bed that is not on the garden plan cannot be planted through the dialog yet.

    Clearing a bed¶

    At the end of the season you clear a bed in one step: in the Planting tab, tap Clear bed while plants stand in the bed. The dialog tells you how many plants and runs are affected and explains what happens: every plant ends with today's date and moves to the history, the running runs are completed, the planting spots are archived and free the bed for the next planting, and the care log gets one entry "Bed cleared". You choose how the plants ended (Harvested is preselected; also senesced, died or cancelled); the choice applies to every plant in the bed. If a plant ended differently, end it on its own plant page first. Planned runs keep their spots. Members from the "grower" role can clear a bed, only while online and not in field mode; it cannot be undone.

    Plant state and plant info¶

    On the Plan tab every standing plant shows its state right on the marker, so you see at a glance whether something needs doing.

    • Disc: the colour is the growth phase, the letter the species. Zoomed in far, an arc around the disc shows how far the phase has progressed.
    • Ring: it tells the state of the planting. Dashed and faint means planned, dotted with seed dots means sown, solid means growing, a double ring means harvesting, and a cross on an empty ring means failed.
    • Sign at the top right: at most one appears, the most urgent: a triangle for an open finding, a hexagon with a star for frost risk, a circle with an exclamation mark for an overdue task, a drop for watering due and a square with a check mark for ready to harvest. All other reasons are listed in the plant info.

    Ring and sign differ without colour too, so you can tell them apart in greyscale and with red-green weakness. The collapsible Legend under the plan explains every sign.

    Plant info. Rest the mouse on a plant for 0.3 seconds and an info bubble shows species, cultivar, phase and progress, age, position, last care, next task and the reasons it needs attention. The bubble stays while the pointer is on the plant or the bubble and closes with Esc. With the keyboard you get the same info through focus on the marker. On a touch screen, tap the plant to open a sheet at the bottom with the cover photo and a Details button. Hovering sends no request: Kamerplanter loads the states of all plants of a bed once, after every action on the bed and when you come back to the tab.

    Not implemented yet

    Frost risk is not shown yet; it arrives with the bed's site features. Watering due currently comes only from a watering task that is due today, not from the irrigation demand. Planned places are still drawn without species and date.

    You see the same discs, rings and signs in the garden plan for the whole site when you switch on the Show plant state layer there; it is described on the Garden Plan page.

    Care on the bed¶

    The Care tab lists every open task attached to the bed or to a plant in it. Overdue tasks come first and are marked red, followed by the other open tasks. With the "viewer" role you see the list read-only.

    Completing a task. Tap Done. A short dialog opens that asks only for what fits the kind of task:

    Kind of task Fields
    Watering, flushing Amount (litres, millilitres or litres per m²) and water source
    Feeding Product (from the catalogue or free text), amount and unit
    Soil amendment, liming Material, amount and unit
    Mulching Material and thickness in centimetres
    Covering, uncovering Material (fleece, net, foil, frost cover)
    Harvest Weight
    All others Note

    Every field is optional, so you can tap Complete without entering anything. When defaults to now; you can backdate up to 365 days, but never into the future. The details go into the bed's care log. A double tap counts once.

    Feeding per m². When you feed and pick a product from the catalogue, the dialog fills in the rate per m² from the product data sheet, for example 80 grams per m² for horn shavings. Below it you see what that means for your bed: on 3.6 m² that is 288 g. For litres per m² the layer thickness is shown as well, because 1 L/m² equals 1 mm. You can change the rate; the dialog never overwrites a quantity you typed yourself. It takes the suggestion only from the data sheet, never from a soil analysis or from how hungry a crop is. If your bed has no area because it is not drawn on the plan, "grams per m²" and "litres per m²" are disabled; enter a total in grams, kilograms or litres instead. Kamerplanter does not accept a rate of 0 per m².

    Nitrogen as an approximation. If the product label states an N content, the dialog also shows "≈ … kg N/ha" for grams per m²: 80 g/m² of horn shavings at 13 % N are about 10.4 g N/m² or 104 kg N/ha (1 g/m² = 10 kg/ha). This is only an approximation from the label, not a nutrient balance. There is no warning when the total of a season gets high.

    Soil amendment and liming. Under Material you pick from a list, for example compost, manure, horn shavings, rock dust, biochar, sand, perlite, coffee grounds, green manure, sulfur, lime or wood ash. Liming offers lime and wood ash. If nothing fits, type your own name. For lime and wood ash a hint appears: both raise the pH. The hint names its source (University of New Hampshire Extension, Alberta Agriculture). Kamerplanter does not calculate how much lime your soil needs.

    Organic soil amendment in the feeding balance. When you apply compost, manure or horn shavings, Kamerplanter counts it as feeding the plants in the bed: it creates an entry in their feeding log, whether you complete a task or record the dose with Log care. Other materials such as sand or perlite write no such entry. If you undo or void the care entry, the dose also leaves the feeding balance. Kamerplanter refuses a dose on a bed with more than 200 plants; record it per part of the bed then.

    Undo. After every action a message with Undo stays for eight seconds. It reopens the task and takes the log entry back. As the author you can do this for ten minutes; the "lead" role can do it at any time.

    New task. New task creates a task for the bed. If you choose Every plant in the bed, each plant standing there gets its own task and the task for the whole bed is dropped. The categories on offer follow the bed: flushing, EC check and pH adjustment exist only for beds with a substrate, hilling, tillage and liming not for pure substrate beds, ventilating only in a greenhouse, cold frame or tent.

    Logging care without a task. Log care records what you just did ("I mulched") when no task exists for it. Plant protection treatments are not logged here; use the plant protection form, because the care log is not proof of a treatment.

    Suggestions. Tasks proposed by the system appear in their own Suggestions section, each with its reason, for example "ET0 demand 4.2 L/m² over 3 days". Each suggestion has two buttons:

    • Accept: the suggestion becomes an ordinary open task and moves into the list above. You then complete it like any other task.
    • Dismiss: the suggestion is closed. No care entry is written, and the task does not count as done. For eight seconds you can take this back with Undo; the suggestion is then back in the section.

    Nothing happens until you decide. If the bed has no suggestions, you do not see the section. Accepting and dismissing needs the role "grower" or higher; with the role "viewer" you only see the suggestions. If someone else has accepted or dismissed the suggestion in the meantime, Kamerplanter shows a message and reloads the list.

    Not implemented yet: generating suggestions

    Kamerplanter itself does not create suggestions yet. Accepting and dismissing work, but in your garden the section only appears once a later version creates suggestions.

    Several beds at once. On the garden plan page, the button Complete tasks on several beds sits above the bed list. You pick a kind of task (for example watering) and the beds. Kamerplanter finds every open task of that kind on those beds and their plants and completes them in one step with the same details for all. One step takes at most 200 tasks; Kamerplanter reports whatever could not be completed.

    Task counter on the plan. On the garden plan, each bed with open tasks shows a number in its top-right corner, red if tasks are overdue. A tap on it opens the bed's Care tab. By keyboard you reach it through the Care button in the properties of the selected bed, or through the number in the "Open tasks" column of the bed list.

    Colour by priority

    The counter distinguishes only "open" and "overdue" so far. Colouring by task priority follows once the plan delivers the priority.

    Sun and shade of a bed¶

    You record how much direct sunlight a bed gets with five tiles. Each tile shows an icon, the name and the definition:

    Tile Meaning
    Full sun more than 6 hours of direct sunlight on 21 June
    Partial shade 3–6 hours
    Shade 2–3 hours
    Deep shade less than 2 hours
    Unknown not recorded yet (default)

    The hours follow the Royal Horticultural Society definition, measured at midsummer. You set the bounds and the reference date per site, see Sun and reach for garden planning; the tiles then show your values.

    • In the bed view: next to the sun exposure in the overview, Change sun opens the tiles. This needs the "grower" role.
    • In the garden plan: when you select a bed, the properties panel shows the tiles. When you select several beds (frame selection or the Shift key), Set the sun for all selected beds sets the value at once. Locked beds stay as they are. You can take the step back with Undo, and it only counts once you save the plan.

    Sun window and leaf shade. On top of the tile you record when the sun reaches the bed: morning, midday, afternoon, all day or changing. The hours of direct sun in summer are optional, a rough guess is enough. The Leaf shade in summer switch notes that the bed is sunny in spring and later shaded by the leaves of trees. You find the fields in the Change sun dialog of the bed view and, in field mode, in the bed sheet (with large buttons, every change is saved at once). The entries appear on one line under the sun exposure in the bed overview. Kamerplanter calculates nothing from them, and leaf shade is a note only: it does not change the site hint when planting.

    Site conditions: frost, heat, wind, slope, access. In the bed view, Change conditions opens a dialog for the frost risk (Low, Normal, High), the cold-air pocket, the stored heat (wall or building), the wind (Sheltered, Normal, Exposed), slope and aspect in degrees and the access sides. Everything is optional and entered by hand; Kamerplanter computes nothing from it except the bed-width hint from the access sides (see below). A bed with frost risk High is always named by the frost early warning, Low never. In field mode the bed sheet has the large chips Moist / Normal / Dry for the site moisture and Windy; every tap is stored at once.

    Compass direction from the aspect. When you enter the aspect in degrees, Kamerplanter derives the compass direction of the bed in eight steps: north, northeast, east, southeast, south, southwest, west and northwest. Each direction covers 45° around its centre; a boundary belongs to the direction that begins there, clockwise:

    Compass direction Aspect
    North from 337.5° and below 22.5°
    Northeast 22.5° to below 67.5°
    East 67.5° to below 112.5°
    Southeast 112.5° to below 157.5°
    South 157.5° to below 202.5°
    Southwest 202.5° to below 247.5°
    West 247.5° to below 292.5°
    Northwest 292.5° to below 337.5°

    As long as no aspect in degrees is entered, a compass direction you set by hand elsewhere stays as it is.

    For technical users

    The derived compass direction is stored in the location field orientation (API and MCP); the interface keeps showing the degrees. The four existing values north, south, east, west stay valid; northeast, southeast, southwest and northwest are new. When site_conditions.aspect_deg is set, the derived direction overrides a value sent with PUT /locations/{key}.

    The hint Bed too wide. When a bed is wider than you can reach from its edge, the overview of the bed view shows the badge Bed too wide under Reach, with a line giving the width and the guide value. What counts is the narrowest extent of the bed: the shorter side of a rectangle, the diameter of a circle. The guide values follow arm reach: 1.2 m when you reach the bed from two opposite sides (north and south, or east and west), 0.6 m from one side. You set both per site, see Sun and reach for garden planning. Two neighbouring sides (say north and east) count as one side. If you have not recorded any access sides, the stricter one-side value applies; record them under Change conditions and Kamerplanter uses your access. A bed exactly as wide as the guide value counts as within reach. Irregular (non-convex) beds get no hint. The hint blocks nothing and is not an error; Kamerplanter does not yet take paths next to the bed into account.

    In the garden plan, the properties panel of a selected bed shows the same fields under the tiles; a change there becomes part of the plan save, and you can take it back with Undo.

    Site hint when planting. In the planting dialog Kamerplanter compares the sun of the bed with the light requirement of the species (full sun, partial shade or shade) and shows a traffic light with plain text, for example "Bed: Shade — Tomato needs full sun". Green means well suited, yellow only partly suited, red poorly suited. Yellow also appears when the bed is one step shadier than the requirement and the species profile says the species takes that much shade; if that value is missing, the text says "Tolerance unknown". If the sun of the bed or the light requirement of the species is not recorded, no hint appears. The hint blocks nothing and asks for no reason. Up to three further hints appear, each only when both the bed and the species have the value: a bed with high frost risk for a frost-sensitive species, a bed exposed to wind for a species that needs support, and a poorly drained bed for a species that tolerates waterlogging poorly. Every other combination gives no hint, and so does a missing or unknown value.

    Frost risk

    The frost risk of a bed (low, normal, high) will be maintained next to the sun. Kamerplanter copies existing frost exposure settings over automatically (yes becomes high, no becomes normal); until then, the frost exposure setting stays the one that counts. The JSON backup of your garden plan contains the frost exposure, so a restored bed keeps it.

    Layers in the garden plan. Sun window, drainage and frost risk can be shown as layers in the garden plan; they are described under Layers and site conditions.

    Plant state in the garden plan. You see the same marks as a layer on the plan of the whole site, with a filter for plants that need attention; see Plant state and filter.

    History of the bed¶

    The History tab has two parts: the timeline on top and the list below it.

    Timeline. It shows for each year which plant families stood in the bed, for at least the last five years and up to ten when a family needs a long rotation pause. Every family has its own colour and the entry always carries its name as well. Green manure and fallow years are marked. The timeline is the basis of the crop rotation check. For pots, tents and shelves that check is off by default; the history is still kept. The check can be switched on or off per bed; for permanent crops it always stays off.

    You find the switch for the check per bed in the Soil tab (see Setting crop rotation for a bed).

    Follow-up crop recommendation. Below the timeline Kamerplanter names which step of the feeder cycle fits after the last recorded year: heavy feeder, then medium feeder, then light feeder, then green manure or fallow, then heavy feeder again. It also lists families and species of the catalogue that, by the crop rotation master data (rotation_after), can follow the families of the last year and whose feeder class fits that step. This is an order, not a fertiliser recommendation: Kamerplanter names no amounts. Families that are still keeping their rotation pause in the bed are listed apart as "still in their rotation pause" and are not suggested. A species without a stored feeder class never appears; where no feeder class is known for the last crop or no species fits, the reason stands in place of the list. The recommendation appears only when the rotation check is on for the bed.

    What stood here? To fill in a year from before you used the app, tap Fill in {year} at the year or What stood here? at the top. You choose the year, the plant family (or a species, which brings its family), the role (main crop, green manure or fallow) and optionally the season. The last ten years up to last year are allowed. A message with Undo follows. You take a filled-in entry back later with the x on the entry, as its author or in the "lead" role. It then stays in the care log as cancelled. Viewers see the timeline read-only.

    List. The newest entries come first, 50 per page; Load more fetches the next ones. The list mixes care entries with planting events: plant planted or removed, run started or completed. You can filter by category, period, plant and person. Corrections are hidden at first: replaced entries and "Position corrected". Show corrections brings them back, marked "Corrected" or "Correction".

    Who may filter by person

    The person filter is an activity profile and therefore restricted. With the "viewer" and "grower" roles you filter only by yourself. The "lead" role and members with the management admin scope can filter by any member. Members who left show as "anonymised" and cannot be filtered.

    Export as CSV. Export CSV on the History tab and on the History of all beds page downloads the care entries the current filter shows (up to 50,000). You can choose CSV (comma) or CSV for Excel (semicolon). The file holds display names instead of accounts, and text that looks like a formula is neutralised. The export follows the same role rules as the list. With more than 50,000 entries, narrow it by bed, plant, category or period first.

    History of all beds opens the care history of the whole tenant at /standorte/historie. It shows the last 90 days unless you choose another period and offers the same filters plus Bed. The plant filter is available once a bed is chosen.

    Photos of the bed¶

    On the Photos tab you keep track of how your bed develops over the season. The photos are listed newest first, each with its date. Tap a photo to open it in full size; the arrows page through the others.

    • Add photo: From the "grower" role, Add photo takes a picture with the camera or lets you choose an image file. You see a preview and save with Save photo. Kamerplanter removes location and camera data (EXIF) from the image on upload.
    • Cover photo: The first photo of a bed becomes its cover photo automatically. It appears in the summary of the bed view and in the bed sheet of field mode. The star makes another photo the cover; tapping the star of the cover photo takes it back, and the bed then has no cover photo.
    • Remove photo: You see the bin on your own photos; the lead can remove any photo. After a confirmation the photo disappears from the gallery. If nothing else uses it, Kamerplanter deletes it for good. If it was the cover photo, the newest remaining photo becomes the cover photo; if it was the last photo, the bed has none.
    • At most 50 photos: A bed holds up to 50 photos. Once the limit is reached, Add photo is locked and a notice asks you to remove a photo first.

    With the "viewer" role you only see the photos. A bed's photos belong to your garden: they are part of the garden export and are deleted with it, but they never go into the JSON backup of the garden plan. In field mode, too, you can give a photo straight to the bed, see Field mode in the garden.

    Setting crop rotation for a bed¶

    At the top of the Soil tab is the section Crop rotation for this bed. With Rotation check you choose whether Kamerplanter checks crop rotation for this bed: Default of the bed type (off for planters, tents and shelves, on otherwise), Switched on or Switched off. The Permanent crop switch takes the bed out of crop rotation entirely, for example for fruit trees or asparagus. Every change applies at once to the hints when planting and to the follow-up crop recommendation. You can change both settings with the role "grower" or above; observers only see the state.

    A permanent crop cannot have the check switched on. If you try, Kamerplanter refuses the change and explains it in a note; nothing is saved. Set the check to Default of the bed type or Switched off first.

    Maintaining the soil profile¶

    In the Soil tab you record: filling, soil type (with a finger test as help if you are unsure of the type), drainage, site moisture, conditions, as well as pH, EC (electrical conductivity) and humus content. If you optionally pick a substrate, Kamerplanter pre-fills its pH and EC. The drainage also appears in the bed overview once you have recorded it. As a viewer (role "viewer") you see the profile read-only.

    If you enter an EC, you also choose the EC method (saturated paste, 1:2 or 1:5 extract, probe or "Method unknown") - an EC value cannot be read without its method, and the form does not save it without one. Only fill in the Target pH (CaCl₂) if your lab states it; Kamerplanter does not derive a target pH from the soil texture.

    Recording soil analyses¶

    Below the soil profile, the Soil tab has the Soil analyses area. From the role "grower" upwards you open Record soil analysis there and enter the values from your lab report, test kit or probe:

    1. When the sample was taken (up to five years back, so you can also enter older lab reports - every other care entry reaches one year back), where the values come from (Lab, Test kit or Probe/sensor) and, optionally, the sampling depth in cm.
    2. For each value the parameter (pH, EC, humus, lime content, Nmin, phosphorus, potassium, magnesium, soil moisture), the value, the unit and the method. For pH and EC the method is required; if it is not on the report, choose "Probe, method unknown" or "Method unknown". Add another value records a whole report in one go.
    3. You can give a content class (A to E) only for phosphorus, potassium and magnesium when the value comes from a lab by the CAL method - exactly as stated in the report. Kamerplanter never computes a class.

    The analysis appears as a care entry in the bed history. Kamerplanter takes the newest pH, EC, humus and lime values with their method into the soil profile, but only in the profile's unit (pH, mS/cm or dS/m, %) and never from an analysis older than the one last taken over.

    History: for pH, EC and humus the area shows one chart each, with a separate line per measuring method. When the method changes from one analysis to the next, a dashed "Method change" line marks it: values of different methods are not comparable, and Kamerplanter never converts one into another. Show as table shows the same values as a table; a screen reader always reads the table.

    Liming likely worthwhile: Kamerplanter shows this hint only when the bed has the filling "Native soil" and a known soil texture, the pH was measured in CaCl₂ and is below the target pH you entered in the soil profile. If one of these is missing, the area says which. Kamerplanter never gives a lime amount - your lab does.

    Changing the bed status¶

    • Planned → Active: possible from the "grower" role.
    • Retire and reactivate: only with the "lead" role.
    • To retire a bed you need a reason, and the bed must contain no plants. The reason is written to the care log.

    Tip: empty the bed first, then retire it

    If plants are still in the bed, Kamerplanter rejects retiring it. Remove or harvest them first.

    Everything that concerns the whole site as a plan has its own page: drawing and gestures, the bed list below the plan, field mode for the phone, layers, irrigation zones, templates, export, backup and import. You find it under Garden Plan. This page covers the bed view of a single bed.

    Frequently Asked Questions¶

    Can I move a slot to a different location?

    Yes. Open the slot, click Edit, and select a new parent location. A plant currently growing in the slot stays connected to the slot.

    What happens if I delete a location that still contains plants?

    Kamerplanter will not allow the deletion while plants or slots are still present in the location. Remove all plants and slots first.

    Can I keep the location hierarchy flat?

    Yes. You can assign plants directly to a location without creating slots. Slots are useful when you have many plants in one area and want to track each position precisely.

    How do I record my own custom substrate mix?

    Use the Create Mix feature in the substrate overview (see Creating Custom Substrate Mixes). There you combine several existing substrates with percentage shares — Kamerplanter calculates the resulting properties automatically.
